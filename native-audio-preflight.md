# Prepare an English dialogue comparison

Updated October 27, 2026. **Live since October 27, 2026, 17:06 Asia/Shanghai; local and live free-flow checks passed.** This document describes the free preparation feature for the existing [English native-audio guide](https://superseedance.si/en/blog/seedance-native-audio-guide#english-dialogue-preflight). The tool is English-only; other published language editions retain their existing guide.

## What you can prepare

Choose one of three sample lines or write one short line for a single speaker. Review two prompts with the same scene, spoken words and settings. Version B adds only:

> All spoken dialogue is in English.

The settings are Seedance 2.5, text-to-video, five seconds, 480p, 16:9, Native Audio on and Draft off. You can copy either prompt, copy a manual review plan, or open the existing workspace with these settings filled in. Opening the workspace does not submit a generation or spend credits. Any later generation still requires the current quote, sufficient credits and the normal account and service conditions.

The 160-character preparation limit and 12-word cue help keep the exercise small; they are tool choices, not model limits or a promise that speech fits five seconds. Empty, multiline, over-limit or invalid input blocks the workspace links without silently cutting the text. A script warning is not language detection.

## Understand the limits

These prompts have not been generated or audio-tested. The tool does not play a voice preview, hear pronunciation or prove that the added instruction fixes a problem. It does not provide a language/voice selector, uploaded recording, voice cloning or lip-sync editor. There is no fixed-seed control for this comparison.

Keep these observations separate when you later have authorized outputs to review:

| Observation | Record |
|---|---|
| Spoken language | What language is actually heard; flag mixed or uncertain speech |
| Exact words | Transcribe additions, omissions and substitutions |
| Pronunciation and accent | Record them separately; an accent difference is not automatically a wrong word |
| Unrequested speech | Whether scene/action instructions were spoken |
| Sound and mouth timing | Unwanted music, silence and visible timing; do not infer these from text |

Keep every attempt, including failures and its actual cost. Two listeners should review independently. If neither variant reproduces the problem, record that result; a few clips cannot establish a reliable success rate. Use a separate audio-editing workflow when an existing recording or exact approved wording is required.
