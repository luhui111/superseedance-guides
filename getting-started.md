# How to use Seedance in SuperSeedance

For current controls and direct template buttons, open the [complete getting-started guide](https://superseedance.si/en/blog/how-to-use-seedance).

## Text to video

1. Open [Text to Video](https://superseedance.si/en/text-to-video).
2. Describe one subject, a clear action, camera movement and the setting. Keep a short clip focused on one readable event.
3. Choose the supported duration, resolution and aspect ratio. Audio and draft settings affect the requested output; confirm them before submitting.
4. Read the studio-credit quote. Submit only after deciding that the settings and amount fit your budget.
5. Follow the task in My Creations. Preview and download the saved video when the task succeeds.

## Animate a starting image

1. Open [Image to Video](https://superseedance.si/en/image-to-video) and upload an image you may use.
2. Describe how the subject or camera should move rather than rewriting every detail already visible in the image.
3. For Seedance 2.5 image mode, use adaptive aspect ratio to follow the input image.
4. Review settings and credits, then submit and inspect the result.

The current image workflow uses one starting image. It does not offer first-and-last-frame pairs or video/audio reference uploads. These steps describe the supported workflow, not a guarantee of identity, text or product-detail preservation in every output.

## Make motion explicit

A useful prompt states the starting action, what changes through the shot, and how the shot ends. Describe subject movement separately from camera movement. The [original prompt collection](prompts.md) shows both moving-camera scenes and fixed-camera scenes with strong subject motion.

## Keep cost and status clear

Generation requires a verified account, an available model and enough credits. Credit purchases remain closed as of October 24, 2026; check [pricing](https://superseedance.si/en/pricing) before planning a purchase. A quote does not create a task or charge credits. Avoid submitting a second job while an earlier result is uncertain. Confirmed failed tasks return the reserved credits; an unwanted but successfully generated creative result is not a technical failure.
